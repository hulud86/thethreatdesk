DreamHost confirms attackers stole FTP logins and webmail sessions

Web host DreamHost says attackers exploited its File Manager from August to October 23, stealing FTP/SFTP passwords and some webmail sessions.

By The Threat DeskNovember 4, 2026

DreamHost, a long-running web hosting company, has confirmed a breach of the server behind its browser-based File Manager. An attacker exploited a vulnerability there and captured the FTP and SFTP usernames and passwords of customers who logged in to it between about August 2026 and October 23, when the intrusion was found.

The attacker also reached some webmail systems and obtained session information for a limited number of users. A live session can let someone read a mailbox without the password or a second login factor. DreamHost says email contents and files on FTP/SFTP servers may also have been accessed.

DreamHost's response

DreamHost says well under 10% of customers were affected and found no evidence that control panel logins or billing details were accessed. It reset affected passwords, ended active sessions, rebuilt File Manager with extra security and is offering a year of identity protection. Its notice was filed with California's attorney general on November 2.

Why site owners should check their files

An FTP login controls the files that make up a website. Even after a password reset, owners should look for files or pages they didn't add.

Who's affected

DreamHost customers who used its web File Manager or webmail between August and Oct 23.

What to do

Change your FTP, SFTP and email passwords, and check your website files for changes you didn't make.
