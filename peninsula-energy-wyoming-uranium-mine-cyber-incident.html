Peninsula Energy halts share trading after a cyber incident hits its Wyoming uranium mine

The Australian miner says an intruder breached the operating system at its Lance uranium project in Wyoming on Friday night. Its corporate systems in the U.S. and Australia were not affected.

By The Threat DeskNovember 24, 2026

Australian uranium producer Peninsula Energy asked the Australian Securities Exchange to pause trading in its shares after a cybersecurity incident at its Lance Project in Crook County, Wyoming, SecurityWeek reported.

What the company said

In its exchange filing, Peninsula said the incident happened on Friday night and breached the project's operating system. Lance is an in situ recovery operation that produces uranium concentrate, known as yellowcake.

  • Administrative systems in the U.S. and Australia were not affected, the company said.
  • It has not said which technical systems were reached or whether production stopped.
  • A preliminary investigation is under way, along with an assessment of how long the disruption will last and what it means for the business.

The trading halt runs until normal trading opens on November 25, 2026, or until the company makes an announcement about the incident, whichever comes first.

What is unknown

Peninsula has not named an attacker, described how the intruder got in, or said whether ransomware was involved. No group has publicly claimed the attack.

Why it matters

Mining has become a steady target. SecurityWeek noted that gold miner AngloGold Ashanti disclosed unauthorized activity in October, and that Dragos counted 19 ransomware attacks on mining organizations between July and September 2026. Uranium was added to the U.S. critical minerals list in 2025, which raises the stakes when an American producer's operations are disrupted.

A breach that touches the systems running a site, rather than just office IT, is the kind of incident industrial operators plan for least and fear most.

Who's affected

Peninsula Energy shareholders and partners, and other mining and industrial operators whose site control networks connect to corporate IT.

What to do

Industrial operators should check that operational networks are segmented from business IT, keep offline backups of control system configurations, and rehearse running or safely stopping a site without its normal systems.
