CISA flags four exploited flaws in OpenSSH, FreeSWITCH, GPUStack and IBM Aspera

CISA added four actively exploited flaws to its must-patch list on November 9 and gave federal agencies three days to fix them.

By The Threat DeskNovember 9, 2026

On Monday, November 9, the U.S. Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency added four vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog. That listing means CISA has evidence attackers are using them. Federal civilian agencies had until November 12 to patch.

The four flaws

  • GPUStack (CVE-2026-102895): an authentication flaw in a tool for managing GPU clusters and serving AI models. Fixed in version 2.2.0. Recorded attacks included creating malicious backends.
  • FreeSWITCH (CVE-2026-116779): a code injection bug, CVSS 9.8, in the open-source phone switch behind many VoIP systems. A crafted SIP message could run FreeSWITCH commands. Fixed in 1.11.3.
  • OpenSSH (CVE-2026-123158): a login bypass nicknamed "SSHole" that affects only certain musl-based Linux builds without PAM, such as older Alpine Linux. Fixed in OpenSSH 10.6p2.
  • IBM Aspera Faspex (CVE-2026-123218): an authorization bypass, CVSS 9.8, that can lead to full administrator access on the file-transfer platform. Fixed in 5.0.19 PL1.

Patching isn't the end

CISA asked agencies to do forensic checks too. An attacker who got in before the patch may have created accounts or credentials that keep working afterward.

Who's affected

Organizations running GPUStack, FreeSWITCH phone systems, IBM Aspera Faspex or some Linux builds of OpenSSH.

What to do

Patch to the fixed versions, then look for new admin accounts or credentials an attacker may have added.
