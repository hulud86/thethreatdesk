Cyberattacks on supermarket refrigeration force European stores to pull food

Overnight on November 1–2, attackers tampered with refrigeration controls at supermarkets in at least seven European countries, forcing stores to throw out food.

By The Threat DeskNovember 3, 2026

Overnight into Monday, November 2, refrigerators and freezers at supermarkets across Europe started warming up. Chains in Belgium, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Sweden, Ireland, Estonia and Finland reported problems, with later reports from Germany, Italy and Portugal. In some stores, units were switched into defrost mode or pushed as high as 50°C (122°F), and staff had to pull chilled and frozen food from sale.

Affected chains included Ahold Delhaize stores in Belgium and Luxembourg (around 40 locations), Coop and ICA in Sweden, and SuperValu in Ireland, which said 13 of its 223 stores were hit.

Not ransomware, but sabotage

Nobody demanded payment. Attackers got remote access to refrigeration controllers and changed their settings, in many cases reportedly using factory-default passwords. It was the second wave in a month. In mid-October, controllers at hundreds of French supermarkets were tampered with, and France's cyber agency named refrigeration controller lines from Danfoss and Copeland.

SentinelLabs says Russian-aligned groups are credible suspects but that attribution remains open. The EU has said it will check supermarkets' readiness for such attacks.

Who's affected

Grocery chains in Europe that use connected refrigeration systems, and their shoppers.

What to do

Retailers should separate building and refrigeration controls from the main network and the internet.
